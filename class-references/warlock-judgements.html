Your patron is one of the twelve: an archdevil or an archangel. Each of them was half of a Judgement until The King of Night cut the six of them apart in The Division, and each kept half of what that Judgement was.

A Judgement was an act together with the measure of that act, held in one being. Your patron kept one of those and its counterpart kept the other, so none of the twelve can finish its own act now. It has the half it kept and no way to complete it.

That is what the pact is for. You are the part your patron is missing, and the work is to finish the act on its behalf.

Shared guidance for all six pacts sits here. Each Judgement’s own page carries only what is particular to it.

Choosing Your Half

At 1st level you choose which of the two took you. That choice sets which row of your Judgement’s table applies to you, and it holds for the rest of your career. It changes only if your patron changes.

Every feature on a Judgement’s page that names your half reads off that row.

A devil half and an angel half of the same Judgement are the same subclass. Two warlocks who took opposite halves of one act are playing the same thing from opposite ends, which is what their patrons are.

Expanded Spell List

Each Judgement grants an expanded spell list on its own page. The spells on it come in pairs, one for each half of the act, and you may choose freely from the whole list whichever half took you.

Level 14 The Offer

Belphegor intends for each archdevil to eat its archangel. A half that consumes its counterpart properly takes back the whole of what the Judgement had, the strength along with the domain, and keeps its own mind inside it. He has put this to the other five archdevils. None of them has managed it.

Your patron can reach for it through you instead, for as long as you can hold it.

As an action, you take on the half your patron lacks alongside the half it holds. For 1 minute you gain the benefits of both rows of your Judgement’s table at once, and whenever a feature of this subclass offers a choice between the two halves you may take both.

When the minute ends you cannot use any feature of this subclass until you finish a long rest.

Once you use this feature, you cannot use it again until you finish a long rest.

I asked a Krotaphian canon lawyer what the twelve would be if one of them ever finished. She said the word for it had been worn down into a prefix of denial about nine thousand years ago, and that this was the only joke her discipline had.

Design Notes

Six pages rather than one subclass for the archdevils and one for the archangels. The twelve are six acts cut in half, so the act is the design unit and the half is a choice inside it. It also means 420 and 430 both route into the same six pages.

The Offer sits here because Belphegor’s plan is the same plan in all six cases. Putting it on each page would repeat it six times, and it is the one feature that is identical across them.

The Offer is deliberately a worse deal than it looks. A minute of the whole act costs the subclass for the rest of the day, which is the closest a feature can come to saying that the Division cannot be undone by halves.

Open

  • Whether using The Offer registers with your patron’s counterpart is unwritten, and it is the obvious hook. A warlock who has held a whole act once has done something only Belphegor has proposed out loud.
  • Whether an archangel patron will permit The Offer at all. Only Neema is on record against killing the archdevils, so Neema is the one who would refuse.
  • Whether a warlock could be made the instrument of an actual consumption rather than a borrowed minute of one.
